# CF Venture Opportunity Atlas

## Executive Summary

**Data as of:** Cystic Fibrosis Foundation Patient Registry 2024 Annual Data Report (published October 2025); ClinicalTrials.gov interventional snapshot retrieved 9 September 2026. **Not** medical advice, **not** a securities recommendation, and **not** a claim that any opportunity should be funded.

### The Question

Where does meaningful unmet need remain in cystic fibrosis after highly effective CFTR modulators, how well is the current therapeutic and clinical-development ecosystem addressing it, and which areas appear most promising for future innovation or company formation?

### State of the Market

CF in the United States is no longer a childhood-survival story first. The 2024 Registry includes 33,989 people. Predicted median survival is 65.4 years (38.0 in 2009). Adults are 61.6% of the Registry. After Alyftrek (vanzacaftor/tezacaftor/deutivacaftor, approved 20 December 2024), CFF estimates ~92% of people are eligible for at least one modulator. Mean FEV1 among ages >7 is 89.1%. IV-treated pulmonary exacerbations fell to 12.1% (35.8% in 2009). *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* among cultured patients is 23.3% (52.0% in 2009). Lung transplants in the reporting year were 61 (249 in 2019).

That is a transformed disease. It is not a solved disease.

### What Has Been Solved

Modulators are a disease-modifying class for protein-producing genotypes. Survival, lung function, infection prevalence, exacerbation rates, and transplant volume all moved in the same direction. Pancreatic enzyme replacement is a mature product class (80.2% still use PERT). Inhaled antibiotics, dornase, and hypertonic saline remain standard of care. Those facts should not be restated as white space.

### What Remains Unsolved

Residual counts use different denominators. Do not add them.

- **2,434** people were ineligible for a modulator by age or genotype; **1,226** ineligible adolescents and adults had Registry data. There is no approved mutation-agnostic CFTR restoration.
- **623** people aged 12+ who were eligible in 2020 had no modulator prescription in 2022–2024. Reasons are mixed (side effects, perceived stability, access). This is not interchangeable with genotype-ineligible.
- **CFRD** is 19.2% of the transplant-censored complications population (~32,492, not 33,989) and 29.3% of adults in that table. Insulin is standard of care. 16.4% of people with CFRD had no treatment noted.
- **Cirrhosis** is 2.7% of the complications population (**861** people). CFF recommends against routine UDCA to prevent advanced CFLD; UDCA was still prescribed in 10.1%.
- Residual lung disease is smaller and not zero: IV PEx 12.1%, FEV1 72.8% among ages 40–49, 61 transplants.
- Mean clinic visits were 3.0 in 2024 (4.3 in 2019). Dornase 80.6% and hypertonic saline 62.6% among ages ≥6.

The ClinicalTrials.gov pull contains **1,209** interventional CF-tagged studies. A title/summary heuristic flagged **98** likely non-CF bronchiectasis records. Rankings never use that unfiltered set. After review, the competitive map has **54** CF map-eligible assets. NCFB programs (ensifentrine, brensocatib-class listed not-CF-primary) stay off the map.

N5 outranks N1 because nucleic-acid restoration is crowded, not because CFRD is a larger causal CFTR gap. Top-three membership **N5, N1, N4** is stable across four weight presets and ±10 percentage-point shifts. Order inside the trio moves. Patient-impact weights put N1 first (79).

Default company-formation cards (with kill criteria):

1. **H1 — pursue diligence.** CF-specific metabolic therapy beyond insulin (N5, 77). Kill if academic SGLT2/GLP-1 studies close the gap or HEMT-era incidence collapses.
2. **H2 — pursue diligence.** CFTR-independent anion transport / ABCI-class (N1, 72). The differentiated N1 bet. Kill if ABCI safety fails or nucleic-acid restoration covers ineligible genotypes first.
3. **H3 — pursue diligence.** Persistent PA or NTM beyond inhaled-antibiotic SoC (N4, 66). Kill if BX004 becomes a broad incumbent or infection keeps collapsing without a new product.
4. **H5 — pursue diligence.** Disease-modifying CFLD, not another PERT (N6, 60). Kill if 861 people with cirrhosis cannot support a trial.

Watch, do not default-newco:

- **H4** residual inflammation vs adjacent NCFB DPP1.
- **H6** adult-era treatment burden as **services, not a drug**.
- **H7** nucleic-acid CFTR restoration: the most important remaining CFTR problem and the worst me-too newco.
- **N2** and **N7** stay scored. They do not get default company-formation cards.

### Competitive Landscape

Industry investigational crowding on the reviewed map is concentrated in **N1** (gene therapy, mRNA, ASO, plus one anion-bypass Phase 1). Next-generation modulators occupy **N2**. **N5** has zero industry investigational crowding units; visible work is academic metabolic agents and a bionic-pancreas device. **N4** is thin after review (BX004). **N3** looks empty of CF-specific industry anti-inflammatories until adjacent NCFB DPP1 is counted — that is why N3 whitespace is scored 3, not 5. **N8** has seventeen auto-suggested behavioral studies that are not map-eligible products.

### Why Now

Three clocks are running at once. Modulators created a residual-disease question that did not exist in the same form in 2019. The Registry is majority adult, so CFRD, NTM in older adults, and treatment burden are adult-era problems. Delivery of nucleic acids into lung is in human CF studies now (VX-522, ARCT-032, 4D-710, and peers). Those clocks do not all point at the same newco.

### Venture Implications

Deeper diligence is most justified where residual disease is countable **and** the reviewed CF industry map is thin: CF-specific metabolic questions (H1), anion-bypass as the uncrowded N1 hedge (H2), and a defined persistent-infection segment (H3). CFLD (H5) is a biology-and-n question, not a TAM overlay. Do not form a company against “CF infection,” “aging with CF,” or “another inhaled CFTR mRNA” as a slogan. Do not treat 33,989 Registry participants as a market.

### Key Risks / Counterarguments

- An empty CF-specific map can mean generics, health-system operations, or an adjacent indication (NCFB DPP1) will close the gap.
- Falling PA and PEx are real and partly measurement-sensitive (fewer cultures and visits since 2020).
- 2,434 ineligible is not a TAM; many are ineligible by age.
- 623 eligible-not-prescribed is mixed milder phenotype plus access, not a modulator-intolerant product gap by itself.
- Complication tables generally exclude lung-transplant recipients.
- The index is a hypothesis. Changing weights moves order inside the top three.

### Conclusion

The modulator era solved the majority protein-producing genotype problem in the US Registry. What remains is a split book: a crowded, high-evidence race to restore CFTR without protein, and a set of adult-era complications whose industry maps are thinner. The highest default score is CFRD (77, Moderate evidence) because the reviewed industry drug column is empty — which is a reason to diligence generics first, not a reason to incorporate. The cleanest remaining CFTR hole is mutation-agnostic restoration, and it is already a thirteen-program field. Company formation, if any, should start from a kill criterion, not from the index.
