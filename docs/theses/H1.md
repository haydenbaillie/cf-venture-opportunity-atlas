# H1 — CF-specific metabolic therapy beyond insulin

**Pursue diligence** · N5 · Opportunity Score: 77/100 · Evidence: Moderate

Not medical advice, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security, and not a claim that any opportunity should be funded. Scores are an exploratory index. Theses inherit the parent need score; they are not a second ranking.

## The claim (HYPOTHESIS)

Adult CFRD is common, insulin-centric, and empty of CF-specific industry drugs.

Adult CFRD is common and the reviewed industry drug map is empty.

## FACT

CFRD is 19.2% of the transplant-censored complications population (n≈32,492) and 29.3% of adults in that table. Among people with CFRD, 16.4% had no treatment noted; non-insulin therapies were 8.0%; about 58% of people with diabetes used CGM. Insulin remains guideline standard of care. There is no CF-specific disease-modifying metabolic product on the approved-therapy table. Do not apply 19.2% to the 33,989 demographic total.

## ANALYSIS

The reviewed competitive map has zero industry investigational crowding units on N5. Visible activity is academic: SGLT2, GLP-1, metformin, dorzagliatin, verapamil, plus a bionic-pancreas device program. N5 therefore scores 77/100 on default weights with evidence Moderate, first among the eight needs, because whitespace is empty and adult-era prevalence is high — not because CFRD is a larger causal CFTR gap than N1.

## INTERPRETATION

An empty CF-specific drug map can mean a missing product or a problem that generics and insulin already cover. The 16.4% untreated row can be a care-delivery gap rather than a molecular gap. HEMT may still reduce future incidence even if today's adult prevalence is high.

## Bull case

Adult-majority Registry, countable residual diabetes, and no CF-specific industry drug on the reviewed map. That combination is why this is the default top need. A CF-specific mechanism or formulation would have a measurable glucose endpoint and a possible pulmonary co-benefit.

## Bear case

Off-the-shelf metabolic agents plus insulin may be enough. Academic empagliflozin, dulaglutide, and metformin studies are the falsifiers. Endpoints that matter in CF (FEV1, weight, exacerbations) are harder than HbA1c. Ranking first is a whitespace result, not proof that a newco should exist.

## Counterarguments

- Generic incretin and SGLT2 drugs already exist; a CF newco has to beat 'prescribe what endocrinology already uses.'
- CFF links CFRD to lung-function decline and mortality, but that association does not identify a CF-specific molecular target.
- The bionic pancreas is a device crowding unit, not a drug, and may absorb insulin-delivery residual without a metabolic newco.

## What would have to be true

CFRD remains prevalent on HEMT; generic SGLT2/GLP-1/metformin are insufficient or poorly tolerated in CF; a CF-specific mechanism or formulation has a feasible endpoint (CGM time-in-range or pulmonary co-benefit).

## Kill criteria

Kill if academic SGLT2/GLP-1 studies show off-the-shelf drugs close the gap or if HEMT cohorts show CFRD incidence collapsing toward background.

## Remaining diligence

- Read out academic empagliflozin, dulaglutide, and metformin CF trials before assuming a CF-specific mechanism is needed.
- Check whether HEMT-era pediatric cohorts are still progressing to CFRD at historical rates.
- Decide whether the product is a CF-specific drug, a CF-labeled metabolic agent, or better insulin/CGM workflow.

## Verdict

Pursue diligence on a CF-specific metabolic question. Do not treat the 77 as a mandate to form a company. Kill fast if generics close the gap.
